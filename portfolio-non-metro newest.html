01 — Executive Summary
Where Network Efficiency
Meets Financial Discipline

Indonesia's Non-Metro logistics network, spanning West Java, Central Java, East Java-Bali-Sulawesi, and Sumatera-Kalimantan, carried a structural cost burden that eroded margin at every node. Across 35+ facilities, rental cost efficiency routinely ran 2–5x above internal benchmarks, with utilization rates critically low at several hubs.


Through a systematic, data-driven network blueprint—combining QGIS spatial analysis, SQL-based volume profiling, and Excel-driven scenario modeling—this initiative closed underperforming nodes, relocated oversized facilities to right-sized alternatives, and split monolithic hubs to unlock coverage efficiency.


The result: a 38% reduction in annualized network rental costs, a 63% improvement in cost-per-parcel at relocated nodes, and a network architecture that positions the business for profitable scale as volume grows across Indonesia's outer corridors.

Not Profitability

The FY25 Non-Metro network was designed during a period of aggressive geographic expansion. While this achieved broad coverage, it left behind a long tail of structurally unprofitable nodes—facilities that could never reach the volume density needed to justify their fixed costs.

04 — Architectural Solution
Redesigning the Network
for Structural Profit

The FY26 Non-Metro network blueprint deployed four interlocking levers, each calibrated to the specific failure mode identified in the FY25 diagnostic. Every decision was anchored to a simple principle: no facility should exist without a clear path to cost-efficiency compliance.

1

Strategic Closures

  • Sumatera Barat Hub — Closed and volume migrated to a neighboring hub within a 20km catchment radius. The node operated at under 10% utilization. Merging coverage eliminated redundant fixed costs while maintaining service levels in the corridor.
  • North Sumatera Hub — Closed post-lease expiry. Volume absorbed by a relocated satellite hub, creating a more concentrated and efficient last-mile footprint in the region.
2

Right-Size Relocations

  • Surabaya Gateway (East Java) — The largest single intervention: relocated from an oversized regional hub to a right-sized facility, reducing floor area by 54% and cutting rental costs by 78%. This single move contributed the largest share of total savings.
  • West Java Corridor Hub — Relocated and split from an expensive facility to a leaner hub plus a satellite node, reducing the cost base by 86% while preserving coverage density.
  • Bali-Sulawesi Spoke — Relocated to a smaller facility, cutting rental overhead by 66% for a node serving primarily as a last-mile delivery point.
  • Central Java Former Hub — A former regional consolidation hub relocated to a right-sized facility. As a delivery-only node post-downgrade, the original floor space was dead cost.
3

Coverage Splits

  • West Java Split — One oversized hub split into a primary node and a smaller satellite, enabling tighter last-mile routing and reducing dead mileage in low-density corridors.
  • Corridor FM/LM Unbundling — Multiple hubs split to unbundle first-mile pickup and last-mile delivery coverage zones, improving utilization density at each sub-node.
  • Consolidation Load Redistribution — A high-volume hub split across three nodes to redistribute consolidation load and prevent single-point capacity bottlenecks during peak seasons.
4

Targeted Expansion

  • Central Java Cross-Dock — A new small cross-dock facility established to plug a coverage gap in a key corridor, reducing dependency on a distant consolidation hub and shortening delivery lead times.
  • Palembang First-Mile Expansion — Identified untapped first-mile volume within existing last-mile delivery zones in South Sumatera. Upgraded two nodes toward multifunction capability to capture new revenue without requiring additional real estate.

The profitability impact extends well beyond simple rental reduction. Each facility decision was evaluated through a three-layer P&L lens: direct rental savings, operational efficiency gains from improved volume density, and revenue protection through maintained coverage continuity.

The closure of two Sumatera nodes eliminated fixed costs entirely while migrating volume to neighboring hubs that gained density—improving their own cost metrics in a virtuous cycle. The Surabaya Gateway relocation alone was the single largest contributor, right-sizing a large regional hub by 54% and delivering a 78% rental cost reduction while maintaining consolidation capacity for the East Java corridor.
